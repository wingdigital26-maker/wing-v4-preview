The Allen problem · 01 / 04

A tight city wedged between two much louder ones.

Allen sits on Highway 75 with Plano immediately below it and McKinney immediately above, and it is compact enough that a search radius drawn around Bethany Drive or Exchange Parkway takes in large parts of both. When somebody in Allen searches for a service, the results are full of companies whose addresses are elsewhere and whose marketing budgets are larger. Being physically here does not automatically win you the local pack, and a lot of Allen owners assume it should.

The compensating fact is that Allen is genuinely a community rather than a commuter grid. It is a city where one high school football team empties the town on a Friday night, where the same names recur on the same committees, and where a recommendation in a neighbourhood group travels faster than any advertisement. That is a real advantage, and it is almost always being wasted, because the recommendation sends people to a search bar and the search bar hands them a competitor.

So the work here is narrower than it looks. Close the gap between what people already say about you and what a stranger finds when they check. Then take the surrounding towns, Fairview, Lucas, Parker and Wylie, where hardly anybody has written anything worth ranking.
