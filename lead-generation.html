01 / 09 · The problem

Two things kill a local pipeline.

Referrals go quiet for six weeks with no warning, and the leads you do get slip away because somebody else answered first. This service handles both halves: we go find the work, and every lead that arrives lands in a CRM that answers it, follows it up, and remembers it a year later.

02 / 09 · What runs

Outreach going out, follow-up coming back.

  1. 01

    A targeted list, researched

    We build a list of the businesses or homeowners who fit what you do in the cities you serve, then research them so the message can reference something real.

  2. 02

    Outreach in your voice

    Sent in your voice with follow-up that keeps going without being obnoxious. Business to business outreach is legal when done properly, with real sender information and a working way to opt out, and we follow those rules on every send.

  3. 03

    A reply drafted on every lead

    Then a follow-up sequence spaced over days and weeks, because most people asking for a quote are not ready to book that afternoon.

  4. 04

    Review requests and quiet nudges

    After a finished job the CRM raises a review request. Old leads who went cold raise a quiet nudge. Both are scheduled and drafted for you rather than added to your list.

Speed and whose lead it is

Why does response speed matter this much?

Because a lead is at their hottest in the first few minutes and cools fast. Somebody researching a roofer at 9pm is comparing a handful of companies in one sitting, and the one that answers while they are still looking usually wins. It often has nothing to do with price.

You cannot beat that clock by hand while you are on a ladder or under a sink. The system never gets busy and never forgets, so answering is one tap instead of a thing to remember. It is the same reason we build the site to capture the lead cleanly in the first place, since none of this works if the form does not feed the CRM.

How is this different from buying leads?

Bought leads are sold to four of your competitors at the same time.

That is why they turn into a race to the bottom on price. The person on the other end is fielding four calls and picking the cheapest. You are not selling anymore, you are bidding.

What we build is your own pipeline, going to people nobody else in your trade is talking to that week, with your name on it. It takes longer to warm up and it is worth more, because you are the only one in the conversation. It also compounds: the list, the messaging, and the reply data live in your CRM and get sharper every month.

08 / 09 · Past customers

The cheapest work you will ever win is already in your phone.

You have served hundreds of people over the years, and most of them are in a text thread, a paper invoice, a QuickBooks export, or your head. They already know you, already paid you, and already trust you, and right now they are invisible. So the first thing we do is fill the CRM: every past customer pulled out of wherever they are scattered and put in one place, with the job we did, when we did it, the address, and how to reach them. That becomes an asset you own, not a pile of records nobody can search.

  1. 01

    A few days after the job

    The customer is tagged with what they had done, and a review request is drafted and queued while they are still happy.

  2. 02

    At six months

    A maintenance or check-in message, so you are the name they already heard from when something needs doing.

  3. 03

    At the one year mark

    A reminder tied to the season, because a roof inspection matters before spring storms and an AC tune-up matters before July.

  4. 04

    Quiet for two years

    A simple we are still here message with an offer worth replying to. The CRM remembers and surfaces it for you, so you get a call from someone who already likes you.

09 / 09 · How it fits

Does automated mean impersonal?

Only if it is written badly. The sequences we write sound like a person because we write them from how you actually talk, and they reference the specific thing the customer asked about. Most people never think twice about it. They just feel like they got a fast, straight answer.

The moment someone replies, the sequence stops and the thread is yours. The system exists to start conversations, not to fake them. You approve every sequence before it goes live, and if anything starts reading like spam, we rewrite it.

How does this fit with the rest of the presence?

Local SEO brings in the people already searching for you and the content keeps you in front of the ones who are not ready yet. This part reaches the people who have not started looking at all, and then the CRM catches everybody either way. Run the outreach, the follow-up, and the past-customer records together and the pipeline stops depending on luck.

Reviews are the overlap: the automatic review request after each job is also one of the strongest signals in local search, so the follow-up quietly lifts your ranking too. Repeat customers leave more reviews, which is one more reason the CRM pays for itself. You can see the engines behind all of it on our work page.
