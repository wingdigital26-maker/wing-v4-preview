McKinney, TX · Up Central from us

There are two McKinneys, and they do not get found the same way.

We are twenty minutes down Central in Plano, and we take on the ranking, the site, the writing, the outreach and the follow-up as one piece of work.

The McKinney problem · 01 / 04

The square earns trust in person. The corridors earn it on a screen.

One McKinney is the historic downtown square, the courthouse, the shopfronts on Louisiana and Kentucky Street, and a Saturday crowd that walks past your door. Trust there is built by being seen, and plenty of businesses on and around the square have a reputation far larger than their online footprint. The other McKinney is the newer city that spread west and north along Highway 380 and out toward Custer, Virginia Parkway and Craig Ranch, where a customer has never walked past anything of yours and never will.

Those two need different work. A business on the square is usually losing to its own invisibility: a profile with no hours, no recent photographs, three reviews from 2019, and a site that will not open properly on a phone. A business out on the corridors is usually losing on comparison: the customer is looking at four companies at once, from McKinney, Allen, Frisco and Prosper, and picking the one that answered their question best.

Being the county seat cuts both ways. It puts a great deal of professional and civic activity inside the city, and it means the phrase McKinney is attached to far more searches than most towns of its size. A page that treats all of that as one undifferentiated market is the sort of page that ranks for nothing.

Proof, twenty minutes south · 02 / 04
