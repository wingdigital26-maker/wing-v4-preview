Should I Run Google Ads or Fix My SEO First?

One buys attention today and stops the day you stop paying. The other compounds and takes months. Here is how to tell which one you need.

Jack Wing Published August 27, 2026

If you need work booked inside thirty days, run ads. If you need a business that still gets calls in two years, fix the ranking. Most owners need both, in that order, and that is a sequencing decision rather than a budget one. The reason the question feels hard is that it is usually asked as "which one works," when the useful version is "how long can I afford to wait, and is my website capable of turning a click into a call at all."

What do ads actually buy, and what do they not?

Ads buy placement, immediately and reliably. Turn them on this afternoon and you are at the top of the page for the searches you chose, in the area you drew on the map, with a budget you control to the dollar. Nothing in organic search comes close to that for speed or precision, and any agency that sneers at ads is selling you something slower.

What ads do not buy is anything that lasts. When the spend stops, the placement stops that day, and you are exactly where you began. They also do not buy any progress on the organic side. Google is unusually blunt about this in its own guidance on hiring an SEO: "advertising with Google won't have any effect on your site's presence in our search results," and "it costs nothing to appear in our organic search results." Its explanation of how Search works says the same thing from the other side: "Google doesn't accept payment to crawl a site more frequently, or rank it higher." The same holds in the map pack, where the local ranking guidance states there is "no way to request or pay for a better local ranking on Google."

An ad budget is rent on a spot you will never own. That is not an argument against paying rent. It is an argument against being surprised by it in year three.

What does SEO actually buy?

The opposite trade in every respect. It is slower, it is less predictable, and it is yours. Google will not promise you a timeline, and it does not pretend otherwise: its SEO starter guide says "some changes might take effect in a few hours, others could take several months." We quote sixty to ninety days for first movement and month four to six for steady lead flow, and that is an estimate rather than a guarantee. Google itself warns you to be wary of anyone who will "guarantee you that their changes will give you first place in search results."

The payoff is that the March work is still working in November. A city page you paid for once keeps ranking. A review you collected in spring keeps persuading. Nothing about ads accumulates like that, and nothing about SEO arrives as fast.

The question that actually decides it

Not "which is better." Ask these three in order:

  • How many weeks of quiet can you survive? If the answer is under four, you are choosing ads, and no analysis of long-term value changes that. Cash flow beats strategy every time.
  • Is anyone searching for what you sell? Ads capture demand that already exists. They do not create it. In a trade where people search the moment something breaks, ads work. In one where nobody knows to look for you, you are paying for silence.
  • Can the page you send them to close? This is the one nobody asks, and it decides whether the other two matter.

The mistake that wastes both

Pointing paid clicks at a website that cannot convert them. Every click is charged whether or not the visitor does anything, so a page that loads slowly, hides what you do, buries the contact form, or looks broken on a phone is not just underperforming. It is metering your money out on the pavement.

This is the honest reason we do not treat the ads question as urgent. Before you decide how to buy traffic, it is worth knowing whether the traffic you already get is being wasted. Fix the page first and both channels get better at once, because ads and organic land on the same website. That is also why the build and the ranking work are one job for us rather than two.
